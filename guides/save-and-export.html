Save projects and choose an export

Understand local storage, recorded takes and video versus vector output.

By Teja Priyan · Updated 27 October 2026

A project and an export solve different problems

Save a project when you want to continue editing the composition. Export when you need a file to share or place in another design. The Project panel stores work in the current browser’s IndexedDB database. It is not an account-based cloud library: clearing browser data or switching profiles can make those saved projects unavailable.

Keep a backup of important finished exports. If a project saves without its recorded take because storage is limited, inspect the saved result before relying on it. A recognizable still export is useful even when a recording is not yet ready.

Choose the file for the next step

Video preserves a timed performance or generated reel. The browser chooses an available MP4 or WebM recording format. GIF creates a compact looping preview at a reduced capture size and frame rate; it will not match every detail of the WebGL viewport. PNG records a still raster frame. SVG exports a simplified vector representation of the glyph outlines, positions and colors; it does not recreate the full 3D lighting or physical simulation.

A repeatable export check

Select the target aspect ratio and a short duration first. Choose the recorded take when you need to preserve an interaction you performed; otherwise inspect the generated reel before committing to a longer render. Keep the page active until the export completes.

Open the downloaded file outside the studio and compare its dimensions, duration and legibility with your intention. If video recording is unsupported, try another current browser or export a still. When reporting a problem, include the output format, resolution, duration, whether a take was used and the browser version. A minimal phrase that reproduces the issue is more useful than a private client project.
