Build a readable kinetic text scene

A focused approach to lettering, material and movement.

By Teja Priyan · Updated 27 October 2026

Begin with a short phrase

Choose Start Crafting and replace the text with a short word or phrase. A few large letters are easier to judge than a paragraph when you are learning the tools. Pick a font that keeps its distinctive shapes at the size where you plan to share the animation. Font choice matters more than adding many simultaneous effects.

Change one thing at a time

Start with the basic grab interaction. Move one letter and watch how the scene responds before changing physics, material or palette. Compare the same composition under Gravity and a gentler floating or orbiting motion. If the movement hides the word for most of the clip, reduce it or simplify the text.

Use contrast deliberately. Glass or chrome can look appealing while making thin lettering hard to read. Check the result at a small preview size, especially with a busy background. Custom fonts can also have different spacing or unsupported glyphs; test every character in the final phrase.

  1. Set the text and font, then settle on the approximate framing.
  2. Choose one material and a limited palette. Keep a readable contrast between letters and background.
  3. Try a single tool or force. Reset or rebuild when the deformation becomes difficult to follow.
  4. Record a short take when you want a specific performance. Replay it rather than judging only the current frame.
  5. Save the project from Project before experimenting further, and export a small test for review.

Know the environment

Typecraft needs WebGL. A device with disabled graphics acceleration, a restrictive browser configuration or limited GPU memory may be unable to run the studio. If the page reports that WebGL is unavailable, keep your existing work safe and try another supported browser or device. A saved project is local to its browser profile; opening the site elsewhere does not automatically restore it.
